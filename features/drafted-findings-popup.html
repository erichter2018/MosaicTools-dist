Drafted Findings Popup

A glanceable, prioritized list of a pre-drafted study's findings — acute lines loud and bright, chronic/incidental lines dimmed — with per-term loudness tuning and one-click deletion.

What it does

When a study arrives with an AI preliminary draft, this popup shows you the drafted findings as a prioritized list before you start reading: findings are classified and grouped Acute → Devices & Hardware → Degenerative & Chronic, with "loud" findings (measurements, acuity terms) rendered bold and bright while chronic/incidental boilerplate is dimmed and italicized. Within each section, findings are sorted head-to-toe anatomically, tailored per study type, and the prior-study Comparison line stays pinned at the top. The idea: in one glance you know what the draft thinks matters, before you've scrolled anything.

How to turn it on / set it up

Settings → Drafting → Show glanceable drafted findings popup (moved from Behavior in 5.5.26). The popup is movable (drag from anywhere), auto-resizes to its content, and remembers its position between sessions.

Using it

  • It appears automatically for drafted studies and updates when the draft changes.
  • Right-click a line to open the loudness tuner: it shows the per-term contributions behind that line's tier, with ▲/▼ arrows to make a term louder or quieter for you permanently. "Add term from this sentence…" lets you introduce your own term (multi-word phrases work), and "Sort order" lets you drag-reorder the body regions per study type.
  • Click the red X (or the finding's text) to delete that finding from the report — the deletion is propagated into the Mosaic transcript editor via CDP.
  • Left-click elsewhere dismisses it for the current case; it re-appears on the next drafted case, and it closes automatically when you sign or discard.

Tips & gotchas

  • Ultrasound "capture" studies (OCR'd tech worksheets) get a flat rendering instead: measurements bold, section labels dimmed, no acuity weighting.
  • Custom loudness terms match whole words only — a custom "cyst" won't fire on "cystitis".
  • Lines beginning with macro/template markers (like "--" or "##") are ignored so stray macro text doesn't show up as a finding. Since 5.5.29 the popup also strips whole lines that exactly match one of your enabled macros, so auto-inserted macro text (e.g. "Please change default statement…") no longer appears as a drafted finding.
