Diagnostics & Data Sharing

Two opt-in sharing switches — app diagnostics and de-identified dictation — that let bugs get fixed remotely. Everything is scrubbed on your machine before upload, and nothing identifiable ever leaves it.

What it does

MosaicTools has two separate, opt-in, default-off sharing switches:

  1. Share app diagnostics to help troubleshoot issues (Settings → Profile) — a one-way stream of errors, crashes, config, and version info, so problems can be diagnosed without asking you to dig up log files. It contains no patient info, no report text, and no record of what or how much you read — by design, it cannot be a productivity tracker.
  2. Share de-identified dictation to improve accuracy (Settings → Speech-to-Text → Data Sharing) — contributes anonymized dictation data used to tune the speech-recognition ensemble for everyone.

Anonymous usage statistics (new in 5.5.29)

Separately from the two opt-in switches above, MosaicTools sends anonymous, patient-free usage statistics — which features are turned on and how often they're used — so development can focus on what people actually use and retire features nobody does. This stream:

  • carries no patient data, no report or dictation text, no name, and no login — only feature on/off flags, app version, and counts of feature use;
  • is keyed by a random per-install identifier that is separate from everything else — it is not your name and cannot be tied back to you or joined to the diagnostics stream above;
  • is always on (it's how the fleet's feature usage is measured), precisely because it is anonymous and contains nothing sensitive.

It is genuinely a usage histogram, not a record of your work: it cannot see your reports, your patients, or what or how much you read.

What leaves the machine — and what never does

De-identification happens on your computer, before anything is written or uploaded. Every free-text string passes through the scrubber first: patient and clinician names, MRNs, accession numbers, dates of service, phone numbers, emails, and even hospital/facility names are removed or replaced. Accession numbers never appear at all — only a salted one-way pseudonym, which cannot be reversed into a PACS lookup. Study descriptions are collapsed to coarse categories. What's kept is exactly the clinical content needed to judge transcription accuracy (measurements, anatomy, findings), because corrupting those would defeat the purpose.

Upload is push-only to the developer's private storage — nothing on your machine can be browsed or pulled remotely; only what the scrubbed stream sends goes out.

How to turn it on / set it up

Check either box (or both) and save; they apply immediately. Uncheck any time to stop sharing.

Why say yes

Nearly every remote fix in the changelog — dead STT providers, update failures on locked-down PCs, the blank-panel scrape bug — was found and verified through these streams, usually before the affected radiologist filed a report. Honest caveat: no automated scrubber is theoretically perfect against free text, which is why the scrubber is conservative, audited, and continuously improved — and why the whole thing stays opt-in.
