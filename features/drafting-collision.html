Alternate Drafting Popup

On a drafted study, the findings you have already dictated are struck through in the popup and taken out of the transcript, so Mosaic's Process Report reads your words rather than the drafting AI's.

What it does

On a drafted study the transcript box holds two authors — the drafting AI's findings and your dictation — and Mosaic's Process Report LLM has no reliable way to tell them apart. That is why it does not always prefer what you actually said.

This feature settles it before Process Report runs. As you dictate, each drafted statement is compared against your words. When you have plainly covered one, it is struck through and dimmed in the popup and removed from the transcript, so only your version reaches the LLM. Statements you have partly covered stay, with a short blue note saying what is still missing — a side, a size, a location — so you can see why it was left alone.

Nothing is invented or reworded. A drafted statement is only ever removed, never rewritten.

How to turn it on / set it up

Settings → Drafting → Alternate drafting popup. Off by default; it is marked EXPERIMENTAL.

Using it

Click a struck-through line to bring it back. It returns to the popup un-struck and to the transcript, and it will not be removed again for that study. That is the escape hatch: if the engine takes something you wanted, one click undoes it.

The strikethrough is deliberately visible rather than a silent deletion, so you can see everything that was taken and correct it.

Tips & gotchas

  • Your editor is the truth. Correct yourself and the old wording stops counting — say "left", fix it to "right", and the note follows the correction. Words you type in after dictating count too.
  • A drafted finding is never removed because you ruled it out. Saying "no aneurysm" leaves a drafted aneurysm standing, flagged as a disagreement.
  • Plurals carry meaning: "pleural effusions" and "in the lungs" are read as both sides, so you are not asked for a side you already gave.
  • It only matches on words it knows. If a drafted finding you clearly covered is not removed, the wording is probably one the engine has not been taught yet — worth reporting.
