Spine Labeling

Get every vertebral body labeled in the sagittal viewport you are already looking at — by a measurement-based detector or a vision AI — then drag, add, delete, flip and renumber the labels before you accept.

What it does

Spine Labeling puts vertebral level numbers on a sagittal spine image as REFERENCE INK — nothing is written to the report. Map the action and press it — since 5.8.34 it finds the vertebral column in the sagittal viewport you are already looking at, with no click on the spine needed. It proposes a numbering (e.g. T12, L1 … L5, S1) that you confirm or correct on screen. It reads the spine region from the study description, so it works on dedicated spine exams and on body CTs whose sagittal shows the spine (a neck study numbers cervical, a chest study thoracic, an abdomen/pelvis study lumbar).

Two detectors, the same on-screen result:

  • Spine Labeling — a deterministic, measurement-based detector. It keys on the endplate edge rather than brightness, so CT, T1 and T2 all work regardless of whether the body or the disc is the bright one.
  • Spine Labeling AI — sends the (de-identified) sagittal image to a vision model and gets the bodies back. The image's top and bottom bands are cropped off before anything leaves the workstation. Fast and inexpensive; the token cost is shown.

How to turn it on / set it up

Map Spine Labeling (and optionally Spine Labeling AI) to a PowerMic/SpeechMike button or hotkey under Settings → Keys & Buttons. Spine Labeling AI needs a vision-model API key (Settings → Experimental). There's no separate enable toggle — the action does the work.

Click-free (since 5.8.34)

You no longer have to click on the spine. Trigger Spine Labeling and it finds the vertebral column in the sagittal viewport you are already looking at. If no image is active, click anywhere in the one you want and it works from there — the old click-to-seed behaviour still applies as the fallback.

Using it

Press the action. The proposal appears with a control bar — since 5.8.35 it sits just above the viewport rather than over the top of the image, so it never covers the upper anatomy while you check the numbering. Everything is editable before you accept:

  • Scroll wheel (or ↑ / ↓) shifts the whole numbering one level at a time.
  • Drag a label's handle to reposition just that one; the ▲ / ▼ chips beside it drag that label plus everything above / below it as a group; ✥ Move all drags every label together.
  • Right-drag a body handle to bend the whole column onto the vertebrae (the far end stays fixed); right-drag a ▲ / ▼ chip to re-space that run (spread or compress the levels) and nudge it sideways.
  • ＋ Add level — click it to turn on Adding levels mode (the button lights up and reads "Adding levels..."). While it is on, every left click places a body and right click undoes the last one, so you can fill in a whole run of missed levels without returning to the button. The rest renumber as you go. Click the button again, or press Esc, to turn the mode off. (Before 5.8.31 this was a drag-the-button-onto-the-spine gesture, one body per drag.)
  • ✕ on a label deletes it and renumbers the rest automatically, keeping the S1/caudal end fixed.
  • ▲ New numbering (since 5.8.37) — start the numbering over. It clears every label and drops straight into placing mode, and the button itself tells you which body to click FIRST: C2 on a cervical, T1 on a thoracic, S1 on a lumbar. Everything you place after that is numbered from it, so a study that came back on the wrong level is re-numbered in a few clicks rather than nudged one label at a time. The region comes from the Auto / C / T / L selector; if the region is still unknown the button asks you to pick one rather than guess, because seeding a lumbar spine with C2 would mislabel every body on it.
  • Bend the column in one drag (since 5.8.37) — while editing, a light-yellow line runs down the column with three grab points (top, middle, bottom). Click and hold anywhere on the image and the nearest one moves; the other two stay pinned, so the top control tilts the upper half, the bottom control tilts the lower half, and the middle one bows the column. One drag replaces three or four individual label moves. The line disappears once you accept.
  • ↔ Flip labels moves the numbers to the other side of the spine. Since 5.8.35 they start on the left of the column; if the spine sits too far left for the labels to fit beside it, they go on the right instead.
  • ✓ Accept commits; ✕ Reject discards. These are explicit buttons — a stray click no longer accepts by accident.

After you accept, the labels freeze into thin click-through ink beside the column, so you can keep scrolling axials elsewhere while you read. Trigger the action again to remove them; they also clear when the study changes.

Smart hide labels (since 5.8.37)

Labels show only on the slices that actually contain the vertebrae you labelled — the way InteleViewer's own spine labelling behaves. Scroll off the spine and they disappear; scroll back and they return. Without it they stay on screen wherever you scroll to, sitting over anatomy they do not describe.

It works this out from the image number InteleViewer prints in the viewport corner ("Im: 42/78"), compared with the slice you labelled and how far the spine reaches through the stack (a lumbar MR is a narrow slab centred on the spine, so almost every slice contains it; a body CT sweeps the whole abdomen, so only a narrow band does). InteleViewer's Text Overlay must be on for that modality.

It never hides more than it should. If the slice number cannot be read for any reason — overlay off, unusual layout, anything — nothing is hidden and the labels behave exactly as they always have. Turn it off in Settings → Spine Labeling to keep labels on screen whichever slice you are on.

Heuristic, AI, or both

You can run either detector directly (map Spine Labeling and/or Spine Labeling AI), switch between them by hand on any proposal ("Switch to AI" / "Switch to Fast"), or let MosaicTools choose: turn on Settings → Experimental → "Spine Labeling: fall back to AI when confidence is low" and the regular action runs the fast/free heuristic first, calling the AI vision model only when the heuristic isn't confident — so the easy cases stay instant and free and AI handles the hard ones (cervical, MR, degenerated).

How accurate is it?

Measured against a review corpus of 157 radiologist-marked studies, on this hardware:
studies where it finds the spine 157 / 157
vertebrae it lands on, of those you marked 95.1%
studies numbered exactly right end to end 99 / 157
time to label a study ~27 ms

(5.8.40, up from 94.1% and 93 / 157 in 5.8.39.) Cervical CT improved most in that release: three checks now look at the neighbouring vertebral bodies before deciding where a level sits, so a mark that drifted onto a disc or a shoulder-shadowed edge is pulled back onto the bone. Each fires only when the surrounding bodies agree, so a level that is already correct is left alone.

Every number above is re-measured on Windows against the same corpus before each release — the labels you correct are what the corpus is made of, which is why reporting a wrong one is worth the ten seconds.

Good to know

  • REFERENCE ONLY — nothing is written to the report, and the proposal is only a proposal: confirm the level yourself before you rely on it.
  • Transitional anatomy (lumbarized S1, sacralized L5, extra or absent ribs) is deliberately out of scope — it counts what is visible and says so.
  • Where the measurement-based detector can't anchor a level, it shows the bodies without inventing a number rather than guessing. MR is best served by the AI detector today.
  • Please report anything it gets wrong — it is young and will make mistakes.
