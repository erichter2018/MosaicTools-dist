Visual Lesion Tracker

Mark the same lesion on several studies and get them side by side — captured at the same physical scale and measured by the same algorithm every time, so a size change is a real change and not measurement variation.

What it does

Comparing a lesion across studies usually means trusting two measurements made by two different people on two different days. This removes that variable: you click the same lesion on each study, and MosaicTools captures each one at the same physical scale and measures every one of them with the same algorithm.

You get a card per click — a zoomed image of what you clicked, the study date read from that viewport, and a measured size — newest first, with the trend across them.

As of 5.9.0 it is joined up with MTRec lesion tracking: a lesion you measure here populates MTRec live, each surface shows the other's numbers, and a measured size can rewrite the size stated in your report.

How to turn it on / set it up

Map Visual Lesion Tracker to a key or mic button in Settings → Keys & Buttons. Nothing else is required.

Settings → Visual Lesion Tracker has one option, for when the study date can't be read off the viewport. InteleViewer draws some viewports' overlay on a low-contrast plate where the built-in reader gets the day and year but loses the month. With the fallback on, one line of that overlay text is sent to a vision model to fill in what wasn't read — never the image, and never the patient block, which InteleViewer paints in the opposite corner and is not part of the crop.

Using it

Trigger it, then click the same lesion on each study you want to compare — any order, up to four. Right-click undoes the last click; Esc cancels. The fourth click finishes automatically, or trigger it again to finish sooner.

On the results card:

  • Drag any caliper endpoint to adjust a measurement. The sizes and the trend update as you drag.
  • The mouse wheel re-cuts a larger or smaller view around your click, and the measurement re-runs a second after you stop — useful when the lesion is bigger than the captured window.
  • Done commits; Close dismisses.

Tips & gotchas

  • Matching is yours, not the software's. It measures what you clicked; it does not try to work out which lesion on the prior is the same one. That correspondence is the hard part and it is deliberately left to you.
  • Lung lesions are sized by the mean of the axes, which is the convention MTRec's Fleischner logic uses — so 3.2 × 2.4 cm reads as 28 mm, not 32. The card shows that same mean with a legend, so the two surfaces always agree.
  • A study whose date can't be read still gets a card; it shows what was read (often just the year) and re-sorts itself if the date arrives later. The card never waits.
  • If the model's answer contradicts a year or day that was read on screen, it is rejected rather than trusted.
