Spine Labeling

Get every vertebral body labeled in the sagittal viewport you are already looking at, then drag, add, delete, flip and renumber the labels before you accept — or press Quick Accept and take the best answer as-is.

What it does

Spine Labeling puts vertebral level numbers on a sagittal spine image as REFERENCE INK — nothing is written to the report. Map the action and press it — since 5.8.34 it finds the vertebral column in the sagittal viewport you are already looking at, with no click on the spine needed. It proposes a numbering (e.g. T12, L1 … L5, S1) that you confirm or correct on screen. It reads the spine region from the study description, so it works on dedicated spine exams and on body CTs whose sagittal shows the spine (a neck study numbers cervical, a chest study thoracic, an abdomen/pelvis study lumbar).

Three detectors, one on-screen result. All of them run on your workstation — no image leaves the machine. The newest one that answers is the one you see, and the S1/S2/S3 buttons on the proposal let you compare them:

  • S3 — a learned detector trained on tens of thousands of vertebrae marked by a radiologist. It needs no click and handles the hard cases (cervical, degenerated) best.
  • S2 — the previous learned detector, kept as the fallback.
  • S1 — a deterministic, measurement-based detector. It keys on the endplate edge rather than brightness, so CT, T1 and T2 all work regardless of whether the body or the disc is the bright one.

Spine Labeling AI, which sent the image to a vision model, was removed in 5.9.5 — the in-house detectors are more accurate, so it was only a setting to get wrong.

How to turn it on / set it up

Map Spine Labeling to a PowerMic/SpeechMike button or hotkey under Settings → Keys & Buttons. There's no enable toggle — the action does the work. Map Spine Labeling Quick as well if you want the one-press version below.

Click-free (since 5.8.34)

You no longer have to click on the spine. Trigger Spine Labeling and it finds the vertebral column in the sagittal viewport you are already looking at. If no image is active, click anywhere in the one you want and it works from there — the old click-to-seed behaviour still applies as the fallback.

Using it

Press the action. The proposal appears with a control bar — since 5.8.35 it sits just above the viewport rather than over the top of the image, so it never covers the upper anatomy while you check the numbering. Everything is editable before you accept:

  • Scroll wheel (or ↑ / ↓) shifts the whole numbering one level at a time.
  • Drag a label's handle to reposition just that one; the ▲ / ▼ chips beside it drag that label plus everything above / below it as a group; ✥ Move all drags every label together.
  • Right-drag a body handle to bend the whole column onto the vertebrae (the far end stays fixed); right-drag a ▲ / ▼ chip to re-space that run (spread or compress the levels) and nudge it sideways.
  • ＋ Add level — click it to turn on Adding levels mode (the button lights up and reads "Adding levels..."). While it is on, every left click places a body and right click undoes the last one, so you can fill in a whole run of missed levels without returning to the button. The rest renumber as you go. Click the button again, or press Esc, to turn the mode off. (Before 5.8.31 this was a drag-the-button-onto-the-spine gesture, one body per drag.)
  • ✕ on a label deletes it and renumbers the rest automatically, keeping the S1/caudal end fixed.
  • ▲ New numbering (since 5.8.37) — start the numbering over. It clears every label and drops straight into placing mode, and the button itself tells you which body to click FIRST: C2 on a cervical, T1 on a thoracic, S1 on a lumbar. Everything you place after that is numbered from it, so a study that came back on the wrong level is re-numbered in a few clicks rather than nudged one label at a time. The region comes from the Auto / C / T / L selector; if the region is still unknown the button asks you to pick one rather than guess, because seeding a lumbar spine with C2 would mislabel every body on it.
  • Bend the column in one drag (since 5.8.37) — while editing, a light-yellow line runs down the column with three grab points (top, middle, bottom). Click and hold anywhere on the image and the nearest one moves; the other two stay pinned, so the top control tilts the upper half, the bottom control tilts the lower half, and the middle one bows the column. One drag replaces three or four individual label moves. The line disappears once you accept.
  • ↔ Flip labels moves the numbers to the other side of the spine. Since 5.8.35 they start on the left of the column; if the spine sits too far left for the labels to fit beside it, they go on the right instead.
  • ✓ Accept commits; ✕ Reject discards. These are explicit buttons — a stray click no longer accepts by accident.

After you accept, the labels freeze into thin click-through ink beside the column, so you can keep scrolling axials elsewhere while you read. Trigger the action again to remove them; they also clear when the study changes.

Smart hide labels (since 5.8.37)

Labels show only on the slices that actually contain the vertebrae you labelled — the way InteleViewer's own spine labelling behaves. Scroll off the spine and they disappear; scroll back and they return. Without it they stay on screen wherever you scroll to, sitting over anatomy they do not describe.

It works this out from the image number InteleViewer prints in the viewport corner ("Im: 42/78"), compared with the slice you labelled and how far the spine reaches through the stack (a lumbar MR is a narrow slab centred on the spine, so almost every slice contains it; a body CT sweeps the whole abdomen, so only a narrow band does). InteleViewer's Text Overlay must be on for that modality.

It never hides more than it should. If the slice number cannot be read for any reason — overlay off, unusual layout, anything — nothing is hidden and the labels behave exactly as they always have. Turn it off in Settings → Spine Labeling to keep labels on screen whichever slice you are on.

Fixed in 5.9.5: at overlay size the slash in "68/130" is the same shape as a digit 1, so the total could be read as 1130 — a different series as far as smart hide was concerned, and it hid labels sitting exactly where you had just put them. A total is now rejected as a misread rather than believed, and the labels stay.

Quick Accept — label anything, keep nothing (since 5.9.5)

⚡ Quick Accept is the yellow button between Accept and Reject. It takes the best answer available and commits it in one press: the newest working detector for the levels, the newest working fitter for the endplates, and anterior forced to the left so the compression and listhesis wording reads correctly without you reaching for Flip.

The point of it is what it does not do. Quick Accept writes nothing — no teaching record, no upload — so you can use spine labeling freely on any study without thinking about whether it belongs in the training data. A normal Accept still contributes, and that is what keeps the detectors improving; Quick Accept is for the studies where you just want the numbers.

Spine Labeling Quick is the same thing as a mappable action: press it and the labels simply appear, with no proposal box and nothing to click.

One detector on the quick path

Quick Accept measures the endplates with the newest fitter and shows its own numbers, undamped.

It used to run two fitters and keep whichever called less — the smaller height loss, the smaller slip. That was dropped: a blended number could not be traced back to a set of corners you could look at and disagree with. One detector's coherent numbers are easier to check, and the accuracy work in 6.0.5 below removes most of what the damping was hiding.

How accurate is it?

Measured against a review corpus of 157 radiologist-marked studies, on this hardware:
studies where it finds the spine 157 / 157
vertebrae it lands on, of those you marked 95.1%
studies numbered exactly right end to end 99 / 157
time to label a study ~27 ms

(5.8.40, up from 94.1% and 93 / 157 in 5.8.39.) Cervical CT improved most in that release: three checks now look at the neighbouring vertebral bodies before deciding where a level sits, so a mark that drifted onto a disc or a shoulder-shadowed edge is pulled back onto the bone. Each fires only when the surrounding bodies agree, so a level that is already correct is left alone.

Every number above is re-measured on Windows against the same corpus before each release — the labels you correct are what the corpus is made of, which is why reporting a wrong one is worth the ten seconds.

Good to know

  • REFERENCE ONLY — nothing is written to the report, and the proposal is only a proposal: confirm the level yourself before you rely on it.
  • Transitional anatomy (lumbarized S1, sacralized L5, extra or absent ribs) is deliberately out of scope — it counts what is visible and says so.
  • Where a detector can't anchor a level, it shows the bodies without inventing a number rather than guessing.
  • Please report anything it gets wrong — it is young and will make mistakes.

Updated in 5.9.3 — it learned what a spine looks like

Until now the labeler measured the image: it looked for the rhythm of bright endplates and dark discs and reasoned about spacing. Two new engines were trained instead, on thousands of vertebral bodies marked by a radiologist on real studies from this worklist. They are small enough to run on your PC in about a second, and nothing leaves your machine.

The labeler now uses the best engine automatically, and falls back to the next one if it is not confident. You do not have to do anything differently.

How much better: measured on studies the engines had never seen, the new one places roughly five marks in six within one level of the right vertebra, against fewer than half for the previous detector. Cervical spines and studies with no usable study description — the two cases the old detector struggled with most — improved the most.

Comparing engines. Buttons marked S1, S2 and S3 appear beside the region buttons. They swap between the three labelers without closing the box, so you can look at each proposal and keep whichever you prefer. The box names the engine you are looking at and how long it took, and if an engine is not confident it now says why rather than appearing to do nothing.

Switching region (Auto / C / T / L) also re-reads the spine in place now, instead of closing and reopening the box.

Still true: it is a proposal, not a finding. Confirm the level yourself, and please report anything it gets wrong — the engines learn from exactly those corrections.

New in 5.9.11 - marking a level for further review. A separate action, Color Spine Levels, marks levels on a spine you have already accepted. Trigger it and click any level to turn it your chosen color; click it again to clear it. Enter, Esc or right-click puts the labels back to normal reading ink. The level's dot, leader line and name all change together, so a marked level never reads as half-marked.

Set the color under Settings, Spine Labeling, Marked level color. Yellows, golds and oranges are refused: InteleViewer draws its active viewport border in yellow and MosaicTools finds that border by looking for yellow pixels, so a yellow-ish mark would be mistaken for a viewport.

The marks are a reading aid only. Nothing is written to the report, and they clear along with the labels when you move to the next study.

Retrained in 5.9.12. Both the level labeller and the vertebral-body outliner were retrained on the studies you marked up through 18 August - 410 labelled spines (was 373), and 77 studies of endplate corners (was 44). The labeller now gets 98.3% of levels right across the corpus, up from 97.1%, and gets every level in a study right 85.6% of the time. The outliner's corner error on studies it had never seen improved from 4.33 to 3.66 pixels. Nothing about how the feature works has changed - only the models behind it.

Improved in 6.0.5 — compression is now measured level by level. A normal vertebral body is not a rectangle, and how far it departs from one changes down the spine. The lower back is normally taller at the front — lordosis — while the mid-back is slightly shorter at the front. The old calculation ignored that and simply compared front height to back height, so it was wrong in both directions: it invented around five percent of compression on every thoracic body, and at L3 to L5 it could not see a real compression at all. A genuine 20 percent loss at L5 came out as 1 percent, which is why lumbar compressions were never flagged. Replayed over the reference set, the correction removed 11 false calls and revealed 2 real ones that had been invisible.

Fixed in 6.0.7 — the prior-impressions window could close MosaicTools. Not spine labelling itself, but it took the whole application down while you were reading, so it is worth knowing it is gone. The window was filled with text before its window actually existed, and the control underneath could fault during that — the kind of failure that cannot be caught, so the application simply vanished. Fixed in 6.0.6 — a normal L5 could be reported as a compression fracture. A vertebra's height was measured as how far the bone dropped down the screen, which is its true height only when the bone stands upright. At the bottom of the lumbar spine the vertebrae lean: a normal L5 leaning 40 degrees measured 77 percent of its real height, and the missing quarter was reported as a fracture. Heights are now measured along the bone, the way vertebral fractures are graded. Checked over 821 vertebrae from 433 studies, the two ways of measuring agree everywhere the spine stands upright and differ only where it bends — which is exactly where the false calls were coming from. Also in 6.0.5: the reporting threshold is 15 percent, raised from 10, which matches the published grading where anything below 15 is normal — you can still change it in Settings → Spine Labeling. Only one number is drawn now, labelled "compression" rather than "ant"; posterior compression is still measured and still written to the diagnostic record, it is simply no longer shown. Spine labelling is also about three times quicker end to end.

Fixed in 5.9.13 — compression percentages on cervical studies. A normal cervical spine could report 40 to 55 percent height loss at several levels at once. The percentages are worked out against how tall each body ought to be, and on a study running from the neck into the upper chest that comparison was being made against a thoracic body. T1 really is about twice the height of C5, so what came out as compression was ordinary anatomy. MosaicTools now reports nothing at all when body height changes with position quickly enough for the comparison to be meaningless — no number is the right answer there.

Also in 5.9.13: the compression and listhesis readouts share one column instead of stepping diagonally across the anatomy, and Settings → Spine Labeling gains separate checkboxes for anterior compression, posterior compression and listhesis. Those change the display only — the measurement is still taken and still written to the diagnostic record.
