Drafted Findings Within Images

Writes each drafted finding on the InteleViewer images while you scroll — in the black air beside the body, on the images where that organ usually is.

What it does

While you scroll an axial series in InteleViewer, each drafted finding appears as a small framed box beside the body, on the images where its organ most likely is. "Cholelithiasis" shows up with the gallbladder; the spine shows below the body; a bilateral finding sits in the middle.

The drafting AI gives no image numbers, so every place is an estimate from anatomy — which organ the sentence names, and where that organ usually sits in the stack. Works on CT abdomen/pelvis, CT and CTA chest, and plain head CT (where image 1 is the skull base).

On X-rays (since 6.1.2) it works differently: the anatomy is found on the film itself, and the findings show only while you dictate.

How to turn it on / set it up

Settings → Drafting → Drafted Findings Popup, then:

  • Show drafted findings within CT images — CT abdomen/pelvis, CT/CTA chest, head CT.
  • Show drafted findings within X-ray images — every plain film. (since 6.1.2)

Each is a child of the drafted findings popup, so that must be on too. Both are off by default.

Each has its own Dim matched findings: off, a finding your dictation already covers is taken off the images; on, it stays, dimmed.

Under the X-ray row, Hide labels at start of study display (since 6.1.4, off by default): every X-ray study opens with its labels hidden, and only the Show/Hide Visual Labels action shows them — dictating does not. Meant for rads whose dictation is on the whole time.

Show/Hide Visual Labels (since 6.1.4) is an action for a key, a mic button or a floating button. It shows or hides the labels on the images, CT and X-ray. Each study starts fresh. Showing an X-ray's labels shows them at once, even over the anatomy, without dictating.

Using it

Click the axial series so it has the yellow frame, then scroll. Brightness and weight follow the popup — loud findings white and bold, quieter ones grey. Sentences the draft wrote on one line stay together in one stack, in their written order - unless a later sentence names its own anatomy: then it gets its own label at that anatomy (since 6.1.6). A follow-up with no anatomy ("Previously this measured 1.8 cm.") stays with its finding. A box keeps its place once shown; it only moves to get off the anatomy, and only outward. Zoom in or out and the boxes are re-placed for the new view.

Click a box to clear it, the same as clicking its row in the popup: the finding is removed from the transcript. Click a dimmed (matched) box to bring it back. Clicks anywhere else, and the mouse wheel over a box, still go to InteleViewer. (since 6.1.1)

In a wide window (W over 800 — lung, bone), blankets and the table light up and look like patient. The boxes stay where the soft-tissue window put them. (since 6.1.1)

X-rays 6.1.2

Each sentence is placed at its anatomy: the heart, a lobe, the costophrenic angle, a numbered rib or vertebra, the ETT in the trachea, a line in the SVC, a knee compartment, an ankle. On bone films every sentence goes in the nearest black air at its height when there is some; on a chest film, only findings at the edge of the body do.

When every sentence fits in black air, off the patient, they simply show. When any would have to sit on the anatomy, they all stay hidden until you dictate — while the mic records they appear, and they hide when you stop. Findings with no place are listed at the top, tagged [unknown position]. Click a box to clear it. Flipping back to an image already seen is instant.

The anatomy comes from FleXray, an open research model from MIT that outlines 60 structures on a plain film. It is not a medical device; it only decides where a sentence is written. Turning the setting on downloads the model (203 MB) once. It runs again after every zoom, pan, W/L change or new image, as soon as the image holds still. Unusual views (a lateral leg, say) can get the bone names wrong.

Tips & gotchas

  • An estimate, not a detection: it tells you roughly where to look, not where the finding is.
  • Axial only. A combined study (chest/abdomen/pelvis, head plus face or spine) gets no labels rather than wrong ones.
  • Boxes avoid MosaicTools' own windows and InteleViewer's corner text. They may sit on the R/L/A/P letters (since 6.1.7).
  • There is always room for two rows of labels above and below the image, and side labels are always wide enough to read (about 20 characters a line), even where that covers some anatomy (since 6.1.7). A row that ends up on the patient moves away from the body when there is room.
  • Only windows wider than 1200 (lung, bone) are ignored for finding the body (since 6.1.7; was 800).
  • X-ray labels on the same side line up on one edge (since 6.1.7).
  • When there is no air left for a box, it goes where it covers the least patient.
  • Labels never sit under MosaicTools' own button bar (since 6.1.6).
  • On a limb X-ray, the flat grey field around the part counts as air, whatever its brightness (since 6.1.6).
  • A finding with no known place is still shown (since 6.1.5): above the body on every image (at the top on an X-ray), with [unknown position] under it in light red. It is shown, not placed - look for it yourself.
  • A finding that names a side keeps that side, even near the midline (since 6.1.5): a left thalamic infarct goes on the screen's right, the patient's left.
  • Image label text size (since 6.1.9) - Settings → Drafting, under the image options: 50-200% of the automatic size, for CT and X-ray labels alike. The labels are placed again at the new size on the next image.
