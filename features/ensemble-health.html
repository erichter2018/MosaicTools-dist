Ensemble Health Light

A green/yellow/red dot on the main bar shows your speech engines' health at a glance; click it for a per-engine drawer with a plain-language reason when something is wrong.

What it does

The most common dictation failure isn't a bug — it's an API key quietly running out of credits, leaving the ensemble running a voter short. The health light makes that visible: a small dot at the far left of the MosaicTools toolbar that is green when all engines are healthy, yellow when one is struggling, red when one is down. Click it and a pull-out Ensemble health drawer lists each engine with its own light and, when something is wrong, a normalized plain-language reason you can act on yourself — "Out of credits — add funds", "API key invalid" — instead of a raw error code.

How to turn it on / set it up

Nothing to configure. The light appears automatically when you're running the multi-engine ensemble.

Using it

Glance at the dot between cases; open the drawer only when it isn't green. The drawer refreshes live and dismisses when you click away. When automatic failover is active, the dot becomes a split circle — the left half is your configured ensemble, the right half is what's actually running — so a swap-in is visible at a glance (hover for details), and the drawer names the backup covering for the degraded engine ("replaced by …").

Tips & gotchas

  • A red light almost always means billing or quota on that engine's account — fixing it is on the provider's website, not in MosaicTools.
  • You also get a toast notification the moment an engine is detected down, so silent degradation doesn't last a whole shift.
  • The same live status appears next to each provider in Settings → Speech-to-Text, plus per-slot Test buttons to verify keys proactively.
