Punctuation & Formatting

Controls how dictation becomes report text — pick a punctuation method for the transcript and another for the final report, plus new-line behavior, radiology notation cleanup, and your own word replacements.

What it does

Between the speech engines and your report sits a text-processing layer you control:

Punctuation method — there are three, and you choose one for the transcript (the box your dictation lands in as you speak) and one for the final report, independently. The same spoken sentence can therefore appear one way in the transcript and another way in the report.

  • Strip all punctuation — no marks at all. Model-inserted marks are removed, and a spoken "period" is deleted rather than converted. Line breaks still work ("new line", "new paragraph" are layout, not punctuation), and so do spoken hyphens and slashes, because "L4-5" and "cyst/follicle" are words, not sentences.
  • Auto-punctuate — the engines decide. Marks and sentence capitals come from the models.
  • Respect spoken punctuation — only the marks you actually say. The engines are asked not to punctuate, anything they punctuate anyway is stripped, your spoken "period"/"comma" becomes a real mark, and the word after each sentence end is capitalized. If the main engine misses a period you spoke and two other engines heard it, it is put back.

The other text rules apply whichever method you pick:

  • New line after every sentence — a line break after each sentence-ending period, for list-style findings.
  • Radiology cleanup — converts spoken spine levels, units, dimensions, and dates to standard notation (say "el four five" and get L4-5; "three point two by two point one centimeters" formats as numerals).
  • Expand contractions — "there's" becomes "there is"; reports read formally.
  • Custom word replacements — your own find→replace table for words the engines persistently get wrong or phrasing you always normalize; case is preserved automatically, and each row can be toggled.

How to turn it on / set it up

Everything lives in Settings → Speech-to-Text, under the Punctuation and Text Processing dividers. The replacements grid has +/− buttons to add and remove rows.

Tips & gotchas

  • Changed in 6.0.18: the old pair of checkboxes ("Auto-punctuate" plus "Auto-punctuate in final report only") became these two dropdowns. Your existing choice carries over: both boxes off becomes Respect spoken punctuation on both surfaces, and "final report only" becomes Respect spoken for the transcript with Auto-punctuate for the report.
  • Fixed in 6.0.19: a slash or dash is never treated as punctuation and is never stripped, in any mode. They build words here — dates, spine levels (L4-5), ratios (123/222), paired terms (stranding/effusion). Previously a lone slash arriving on its own could be dropped, which is what made some dictated dates disappear.
  • Strip removes marks, not capitals. A word the engines already capitalized stays capitalized, because blindly lowercasing would also hit CABG, L4 and Crohn.
  • Replacements are word-for-word and deterministic; for biasing recognition toward a hard term, use keyterms instead.
  • These rules run identically whether Turbo Paste is on or off — they're applied to whatever the ensemble produces.
