Mic Gain Calibrator

A built-in tool that listens to you dictate and recommends the right Windows microphone gain — so the speech engines get clean audio, neither too quiet nor clipping.

What it does

Recognition accuracy starts at the microphone. A gain set too low buries your voice in the noise floor; too high and loud syllables clip and garble. The Mic Gain Calibrator opens a window that captures your microphone live, shows your speech levels as you dictate a few sentences, and computes a Recommended Windows Mic Gain percentage for your specific mic and voice. You then set that value in Windows sound settings (or confirm it already matches).

How to turn it on / set it up

Settings → Profile → Mic Gain Calibrator. It grants itself microphone access and starts listening; no configuration needed. (It runs on the WebView2 runtime, which is present on any machine running Mosaic.)

Using it

Open it once when you first set up custom dictation, speak the way you actually dictate — normal reading-room volume, normal distance from the mic — and read off the recommended gain. Re-run it whenever your audio situation changes: a new microphone, a new headset, a new workstation, or if colleagues' transcripts suggest your levels drifted.

Tips & gotchas

  • Calibrate with the microphone you actually dictate with, positioned as you actually use it — calibrating a desk mic from six inches and then dictating from two feet defeats the point.
  • The free on-device medASR engine is the most sensitive to audio quality of all the engines; a correct gain (and a wired mic rather than Bluetooth) is what keeps it a strong voter.
  • If the window comes up blank, the WebView2 runtime is missing — the dialog links to Microsoft's download page.
