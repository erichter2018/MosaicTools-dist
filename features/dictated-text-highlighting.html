Dictated Text Highlighting

Tints the words you dictated in the editor so they stand out from template text, with color tiers flagging the words the transcription ensemble was least sure about.

What it does

When you dictate through Custom STT, the text MosaicTools inserts gets a subtle background tint in the editor, so you can always tell your own words apart from template boilerplate and typed text. On top of that, words are colored in a three-tier confidence system: normal, medium, and low. The tiers are driven by how much the whole transcription ensemble agreed on each word — not just the primary engine's self-reported confidence. A word the primary engine was confident about but the other engines disagreed with gets flagged for a look; pure formatting differences ("cm" vs "centimeters", hyphenation) don't flag.

How to turn it on / set it up

  1. Settings → Experimental → check Use CDP for Mosaic interaction.
  2. Check Highlight dictated text underneath it.
  3. Requires Custom STT to be your dictation path — this highlights what the ensemble inserted.

Using it

Dictate normally; skim for the low-confidence colors before you process or sign. That's the whole workflow — the coloring points your eyes at the two or three words per report worth double-checking. The in-editor mode buttons at the bottom-right of the report editor (No Highlight / Regular / Rainbow) control what's shown: Regular is this confidence tinting, No Highlight hides all dictation highlights (Mosaic's native ones too), and Rainbow switches to findings↔impression correlation.

Tips & gotchas

  • Low-confidence coloring stays put in the transcript box after Process Report and survives your edits — the tiers re-map as the text moves.
  • The tint is intentionally subtle; if you don't see it, check that CDP is connected (Settings → Experimental → CDP Status shows a green Mosaic dot).
  • Your chosen highlight mode persists across restarts and study changes.
