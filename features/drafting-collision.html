Alternate Drafting Popup

On a drafted study, the findings you have already dictated are struck through in the popup and taken out of the transcript, so Mosaic's Process Report reads your words rather than the drafting AI's.

What changed in 6.0.11

A note can only quote your draft's own words. "Mild hazy and diffuse perihilar lung opacities" could come back annotated "you didn't state the atelectasis" — a word that is nowhere in that sentence. Inside the matcher, "opacities" belongs to the airspace family, and that family's internal label happens to be "atelectasis"; the note printed the label. It now quotes the wording the draft actually used, or says nothing. Where a draft really does say atelectasis, it is still named.

What changed in 6.0.10

Words for the same thing. "mid-pole" and "midpole" are now one place — a single hyphen used to make the popup say you had not stated a location when you had. A spinal fusion, a laminectomy and a decompression are now recognised as surgery; before this a drafted "C3 through C6 posterior spinal fusion" carried no finding the matcher could read, so it could never meet what you said. And an appendicolith the draft calls 1 cm while you call it 7 mm is treated as one stone measured twice, not as a disagreement — the same rule that already applied to gallstones.

A drafted "bilateral" is answered by the one side you named — for spread findings such as basilar scarring, atelectasis, consolidation, interstitial opacities and renal cysts. Describing the abnormality on one side is enough; you are not expected to add "and the other one too".

Pleural effusions are deliberately left out of that rule. A second effusion is a second fluid collection and a second decision, so a drafted bilateral effusion still comes to you when you named one side.

Hardware statements are safer. On a chest radiograph the matcher works from body part alone, and every device on that film is the same body part — so any line or tube you mentioned could clear an unrelated one. A draft stating that wires are INTACT or ALIGNED is a claim that they were examined and found whole; that is now kept unless you say so yourself. A draft that merely notes hardware is present still clears as before.

What changed in 6.0.9

It knows a lot more anatomy. The deep brain (thalamus, caudate, brainstem, the coverings of the brain), heart valves, the chest wall, the pharynx, teeth, surgical sutures and staples, and about sixty more terms. A statement the matcher could put no body part to could never pair with anything you said, so it simply stood. A bladder diverticulum is also no longer filed as bowel.

It stops asking for things you already said. If you restate a finding with your own measurement, an extra location word in the draft no longer counts as a gap. A size you gave in words ("a tiny diverticulum") is reported as a disagreement with the draft rather than as silence. And a drafted line naming two findings can now be answered by two of your sentences instead of one — "small bilateral pleural effusions with mild pulmonary edema" is covered by saying the edema in one breath and the effusions in the next.

Two ways it could clear the wrong statement are closed. A plural organ belonging to a different organ no longer answers the draft's side, and the word "both" borrowed from a neighbouring sentence no longer does either.

Removed comparisons are ignored entirely. Clearing a comparison line is housekeeping, not a finding, and it no longer teaches the matcher anything.

What changed in 6.0.3

One dictated sentence can no longer be split across two drafted findings. Where a report drafts several of the same lesion — three lung nodules, say — each drafted line is now read against a single one of your sentences. Before, a drafted right LOWER lobe nodule could be struck out by taking "right" from your middle-lobe nodule and "lower lobe" from your left-sided one, and on one study the word "bilateral" was taken from a breast-implant line to supply the side for a lung nodule.

Levels that are different operations now have to agree: suprarenal and infrarenal aortic aneurysms no longer clear each other, nor do thoracic and abdominal ones. A general statement no longer clears a specific vertebra — "fractures throughout the mid and lower thoracic spine" leaves a drafted "severe T5 compression fracture" standing.

On X-rays, one sentence no longer clears every drafted line about that body part. Dictating a knee effusion was striking out severe osteoarthritis.

More of your words are understood: the entire colon covers the right colon, TIPS, colectomy, the whole pneumo- family (and none of them can clear another — gas in one place is not gas in another), the lingula as left and the middle lobe as right, gynecomastia, anterolisthesis and pars defects, "grade 1" as a size, decompressed = contracted, and "effusions" covering a draft that named one side.

What it does

On a drafted study the transcript box holds two authors — the drafting AI's findings and your dictation — and Mosaic's Process Report LLM has no reliable way to tell them apart. That is why it does not always prefer what you actually said.

This feature settles it before Process Report runs. As you dictate, each drafted statement is compared against your words. When you have plainly covered one, it is struck through and dimmed in the popup and removed from the transcript, so only your version reaches the LLM. Statements you have partly covered stay, with a short blue note saying what is still missing — a side, a size, a location — so you can see why it was left alone.

Nothing is invented or reworded. A drafted statement is only ever removed, never rewritten.

How to turn it on / set it up

Settings → Drafting → Alternate drafting popup. Off by default; it is marked EXPERIMENTAL.

Using it

Click a struck-through line to bring it back. It returns to the popup un-struck and to the transcript, and it will not be removed again for that study. That is the escape hatch: if the engine takes something you wanted, one click undoes it.

The strikethrough is deliberately visible rather than a silent deletion, so you can see everything that was taken and correct it.

Fixed in 6.0.1

  • A finding no longer appears twice when MosaicTools corrects the draft itself. An auto-corrected ETT or central-line position was being counted as something you had dictated, so the original line stayed on screen beside the correction.
  • A size stated in words counts as a size. "Moderate left frontal scalp hematoma" no longer asks you for a measurement you already gave.
  • A different location is named, not called missing. Where the draft says the lower lobe and you said the upper lobe, the note now says so instead of claiming you stated no location.
  • Both sides said in two sentences counts as both sides — "left adrenal nodule… right adrenal nodule…" no longer asks for the other side.
  • Many more words understood: every bone in adjective form, encephalomalacia and the MCA/ACA/PCA territories, sternotomy and CABG, cirrhosis, prostatomegaly, gastric banding, and the whole tube and drain family. A "transverse" fracture is no longer read as the transverse colon.

Stating a size counts as covering the statement 6.0.6

When you replace a drafted call with a size of your own — "the mid-ascending thoracic aorta is borderline in size at 4.1 cm" against a drafted "Aneurysmal ascending thoracic aorta, maximum diameter 4.1 cm" — that now counts. Same vessel, same measurement, and you are deliberately downgrading the draft's wording, so the drafted sentence is taken out and yours stands alone. Before 6.0.6 the two shared no finding word, so the overcall was left in the report beside your weaker one.

The phrases that count are the explicit size statements: borderline in size, borderline in caliber, borderline enlarged, borderline dilated, upper limits of normal. The bare word "borderline" does not — it attaches to anything ("borderline suspicious", "borderline compressed") and would clear far too much.

A central line is not a chest tube 6.0.8

On a portable chest film the check runs in a looser mode, because chest dictation is terse and often uses words the engine cannot read. In that mode any hardware statement matched any other, so dictating about one device could clear — or raise a side conflict against — a drafted statement about a different one. A "Right central line tip is in the mid to lower SVC." was matched to a drafted "Two left chest tubes."

Lines and tubes now have to be the same device: endotracheal tube, enteric tube, central line, chest tube, urinary drain. Each of those is placed in a patient and carries its own position statement, and one can never speak for another.

Everything else hardware does is unchanged and still matches on region alone — sternotomy wires, pacemakers, prostheses, and the bare words "catheter" and "drain", which say nothing about which device they are.

New equivalences in this release: renal collecting system fullness = hydronephrosis, fat density adrenal nodule = myelolipoma, lobulated liver contour = nodular liver = cirrhosis, proximal colon = right colon, and the lingula as part of the left upper lobe.

Tips & gotchas

  • Your editor is the truth. Correct yourself and the old wording stops counting — say "left", fix it to "right", and the note follows the correction. Words you type in after dictating count too.
  • A drafted finding is never removed because you ruled it out. Saying "no aneurysm" leaves a drafted aneurysm standing, flagged as a disagreement.
  • Plurals carry meaning: "pleural effusions" and "in the lungs" are read as both sides, so you are not asked for a side you already gave.
  • It only matches on words it knows. If a drafted finding you clearly covered is not removed, the wording is probably one the engine has not been taught yet — worth reporting.
