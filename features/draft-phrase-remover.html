Auto-Remove Draft Phrases

Keep a checklist of exact statements the drafting AI keeps inserting that you never want, and MosaicTools deletes them from the draft automatically when the study opens.

What it does

The drafting AI sometimes drops in the same throwaway line study after study — a boilerplate statement that's true-but-useless and that you'd otherwise delete by hand every time. This lets you list those exact statements once. When a drafted (or capture) study opens, MosaicTools scans the draft and removes any statement on your list that appears verbatim, before you read it and before Process Report runs — so it never reaches the Final Report.

Each phrase has its own checkmark, so you can keep a phrase in the list but switch it off without deleting it.

How to turn it on / set it up

Go to Settings → Drafting → Auto-remove draft phrases (moved from Report Display in 5.5.26).

  • Add… — paste the statement exactly as the AI writes it, including the trailing period. New entries start checked (active).
  • Checkmark — check to make a phrase active, uncheck to disable it while keeping it in the list.
  • Edit… (new in 5.6.1) — reopen the selected phrase pre-filled and change it in place; it keeps its position in the list and its checkmark, so a typo or a slightly-off wording no longer means removing and re-adding.
  • Remove — delete the selected phrase from the list entirely.

One phrase ships pre-loaded and unchecked as an example: "Calcified atherosclerosis at the skull base." Check it if you want it removed, or add your own.

Using it

Open a drafted study. Any active phrase found verbatim in the draft is gone by the time you look — no extra step. It runs once per study, and the drafted-findings box shows the already-cleaned draft.

Tips & gotchas

  • The match is exact and case-sensitive — it only removes the statement you typed, character-for-character (including the period). If the AI's wording varies, add each variant as its own entry.
  • It removes the statement text surgically; on current Mosaic a removed full-sentence line may briefly leave a blank line in the draft, which collapses when the report is processed.
  • It targets the AI draft (the transcript editor), not your own dictation — anything you say is never touched.
