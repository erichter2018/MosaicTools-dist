Drafted Findings Popup

A glanceable, prioritized list of a pre-drafted study's findings — acute lines loud and bright, chronic/incidental lines dimmed — with per-term loudness tuning, one-click deletion, and an organ-rail layout that puts the body part at the left edge.

The IMPRESSION light 6.0.21

The -- IMPRESSION -- rule above the impression block is a traffic light.

Colour What it means
Green Process Report has run on this study and you have not said anything since. The report matches your dictation.
Red There is dictation the report has not caught up with. Press Process Report.
Grey No claim. MosaicTools cannot see the report, the study is an addendum, or you have not dictated here.

Red appears a beat after you speak a new word, so it is on screen while you are deciding whether to process again. It also goes red when a macro lands in the transcript, because that text is not in the report either.

(6.0.22) Green also needs every prior date on the transcript's COMPARISON line to be in the report. When MosaicTools adds the priors just after Mosaic has already processed the study, the report can still say "None available" — the light is now red until you process again.

Grey is deliberately not a middle grade. Green only appears when it has been earned — a green rule you did not earn would be worse than no rule at all.

Its own font size 6.0.12

Hover anywhere over the popup and two small buttons appear in its top right corner, sharing the first line: A− and A+. Each press changes the size by half a point.

This is the popup's own control. It does not touch Mosaic's font size, your report, or Windows display scaling — only this box. The size is remembered between studies and between sessions.

The emphasis is preserved at every size. Loud, normal, quiet and chronic lines are separated by fixed point gaps, so an important finding stays exactly as much louder than a normal one when the whole popup is larger. Turning the size up does not flatten the tiers, and turning it down does not exaggerate them.

Holding a fixed height 6.0.12

By default the popup grows and shrinks to fit the findings, so a long study makes a tall box and a short one makes a small box. If you would rather it stayed put, tick Settings → Drafting → Fixed height (do not auto-expand vertically).

With that on, the bottom edge becomes a resize grip — the same invisible strip the left and right edges already use, so there is nothing drawn; the cursor changes when you are on it. Drag it to the height you want. Studies with more findings than fit will scroll inside the box.

The height is remembered exactly like the width, including per modality if you use Remember a separate position for each modality.

Getting it back after you close it 6.0.3

Closing the popup keeps it closed for the rest of that study — deliberate, so a later change to the drafted text cannot make it reappear over your reading. That is right when you meant to close it and a dead end when the click was a stray.

Since 6.0.3, closing it puts a small blue drafted box next to the word Transcript in Mosaic. Click the box and the popup comes back. It is not there while the popup is open, and it disappears again when you move to the next study.

Show, Hide and Orig 6.0.15

Two small buttons sit to the left of the A− / A+ pair, and appear on hover just as those do.

Show / Hide appears when matched entries are being removed from the popup and "Show matched on mouseover" is off. Press Show and the removed entries come back, struck through, and stay — they do not vanish when you move the mouse away. Press Hide to put them away again. Every study starts hidden.

That checkbox now picks between two ways of seeing the same rows rather than being on-or-nothing: on gives the hover peek, off gives the Show button.

Orig appears only on a study where the spine splitter actually rewrote a line, so the button being there is itself the answer to "did it fire?". Press it and a read-only panel shows the sentence the draft arrived with, the lines it became, and what has happened to each of those lines since — still in the report, matched for removal, or removed by you. Nothing on that panel changes your report.

What it does

When a study arrives with an AI preliminary draft, this popup shows you the drafted findings as a prioritized list before you start reading: findings are classified and grouped Acute → Devices & Hardware → Degenerative & Chronic, with "loud" findings (measurements, acuity terms) rendered bold and bright while chronic/incidental boilerplate is dimmed and italicized. Within each section, findings are sorted head-to-toe anatomically, tailored per study type, and the prior-study Comparison line stays pinned at the top. The idea: in one glance you know what the draft thinks matters, before you've scrolled anything.

How to turn it on / set it up

Settings → Drafting → Show glanceable drafted findings popup (moved from Behavior in 5.5.26). The popup is movable (drag from anywhere), auto-resizes to its content, and remembers its position between sessions.

Remember a separate position for each modality (new in 5.9.7, off by default) — underneath the main toggle. Off, one remembered position is used for every study. On, the popup's position is remembered per modality (CT, MR, US, XR, NM, MG, PET) and it moves to that modality's spot as studies change; CTA counts as CT, and MRA/MRV as MR. A modality you have not placed yet leaves the popup where it is rather than jumping, and the next time you drag it there that spot is claimed. Turning the option back off returns the popup to the single shared position, and your per-modality layout is kept in case you turn it on again.

Using it

  • It appears automatically for drafted studies and updates when the draft changes.
  • Right-click a line to open the loudness tuner: it shows the per-term contributions behind that line's tier, with ▲/▼ arrows to make a term louder or quieter for you permanently. "Add term from this sentence…" lets you introduce your own term (multi-word phrases work), and "Sort order" lets you drag-reorder the body regions per study type.
  • Click the red X (or the finding's text) to delete that finding from the report — the deletion is propagated into the Mosaic transcript editor via CDP.
  • Left-click elsewhere dismisses it for the current case; it re-appears on the next drafted case, and it closes automatically when you sign or discard.

Abnormal measurement colors (since 5.8.32)

Measurements are checked against published normal ranges and colored by how they sit against them:

  • Light pink — above the accepted upper limit.
  • Red — well above it (a per-measurement threshold: aorta at 5.0 cm, approaching the repair threshold; liver at 18 cm; otherwise 50% over).
  • Light blue / deeper blue — below a lower limit. A thinned renal cortex or a low portal vein velocity matters as much as a high number, so these are called out too.

Covered to start (abdominal / right-upper-quadrant ultrasound): liver length, gallbladder length and wall thickness, common bile duct, kidney length, renal cortical thickness, aorta, portal vein diameter and velocity, spleen, pancreatic duct, IVC. The common bile duct limit adjusts for age (it widens about 1 mm per decade after 60) and lifts further if the report mentions a prior cholecystectomy.

An uncolored number means there is no rule for it — not that it is normal. Only the measurements listed above are checked, and a measurement of a mass, cyst, nodule or stone is deliberately never colored, because a lesion has no normal range.

Turn it off in Settings → Drafting → "Color abnormal measurements in the popup".

The organ-rail layout 6.0.9

This is simply how the popup looks — there is no toggle. As of 6.0.9 it is the one and only appearance, so turning on Show glanceable drafted findings popup gives you this. (It was an optional "Alternate appearance" checkbox from 5.9.20; that checkbox is gone and the layout is now the default.)

Instead of a block of sentences, each finding gets its body part as a coloured tag in a fixed left column, so the left edge of the box becomes a list of anatomy you can scan without reading a word. "Did I say anything about the kidneys?" is answered by looking, not reading.

Three things ride along with it:

  • A red bar down the left of the loud findings.
  • Measurements bold and bright, so a size is the thing your eye lands on.
  • A whispered tail. A throwaway qualifier — "…, too small to characterize, probably a benign cyst." — drops to a small grey. On a typical abdomen that boilerplate was about a third of the lit pixels while adding nothing you act on.

Nothing is reworded, reordered differently, or removed. A hedge is only ever dimmed, never deleted, only when it fully matches a short list of harmless qualifiers, and never on a loud finding or one that says something like "cannot exclude" or "suspicious for".

The columns are fixed, so they do not shift as you move between studies, and the box stays where you put it — a study too tall for the space scrolls rather than moving. You can drag its left or right edge to change the width; the width is remembered.

Sentences dictated together stay together (since 5.9.20)

The drafting AI writes one organ system per line — a chest line, a renal line, a spine line. The popup used to split every line into sentences and re-sort each one on its own, so half a chest paragraph could end up under "Degenerative & Chronic" while the other half sat at the top, and an unrelated sentence could land in the middle of a group.

Now a paragraph moves as a unit: it is filed under the section of its most urgent member, and sorts at the position of its body part. Each sentence still gets its own line, in the order it was drafted.

If a draft arrives as a single paragraph there is nothing to hold together, so it falls back to the previous sentence-by-sentence ordering.

The comparison line stays current 6.0.6

The popup takes its picture of the draft when the study opens — but the COMPARISON: line is often written a few seconds later, and you may pull another prior with Get Prior halfway through the case. It now follows that line for the whole case instead of showing the one that happened to be there at the start. A comparison that arrives late is no longer mistaken for something you dictated, either.

Tips & gotchas

  • Ultrasound "capture" studies (OCR'd tech worksheets) get a flat rendering instead: measurements bold, section labels dimmed, no acuity weighting.
  • Custom loudness terms match whole words only — a custom "cyst" won't fire on "cystitis".
  • Lines beginning with macro/template markers (like "--" or "##") are ignored so stray macro text doesn't show up as a finding. Since 5.5.29 the popup also strips whole lines that exactly match one of your enabled macros, so auto-inserted macro text (e.g. "Please change default statement…") no longer appears as a drafted finding.
