Text Rules

One list of find-and-replace rules covering both the text the AI drafted and the words you dictate or type. Leave the replacement blank and the rule deletes what it finds instead.

One list instead of three 6.0.20

Until 6.0.20 there were three separate lists doing the same job in different places: auto-remove draft phrases and find-and-replace in drafts, both under Drafting, and a word-replacement table for dictation under Text & Templates. A correction you wanted in both your drafts and your own speech had to be typed twice, in two different settings pages, and the two copies drifted apart.

They are now one list. Your existing entries were moved across automatically the first time you ran 6.0.20 — nothing to re-enter, and nothing was thrown away.

The two switches that turn the rules on moved too. They sit at the top of the Text Rules editor, right above the list they control, instead of being split across two settings pages.

What it does

A text rule finds something and either replaces it or deletes it. Each rule says where it applies — the text the drafting AI wrote, the words you dictate or type, or both — and how it matches. One rule can strip the boilerplate sentence your drafting AI keeps inserting, and another can turn "ptx" into "pneumothorax" the instant you finish saying it.

How to turn it on / set it up

  1. Open Settings → Drafting → Text rules, or Settings → Text & Templates → Text rules — both buttons open the same editor and the same list.
  2. Tick Apply rules to drafted text to let rules edit the AI's draft. This is off by default, because these are the only rules that delete or rewrite text in your report on their own.
  3. Tick Apply rules live as I type or dictate to have a rule fire the moment you finish a word — space, comma, period or Enter. Apply to chooses the transcript box, the final report, or both.
  4. Click Add… and fill in Find, Replace, where it applies, and how it matches. Leave Replace blank and the rule deletes what it finds.

Using it

Three match modes:

  • Whole word — finds your text as a word anywhere, ignoring capitals, and carries your capitals across, so "ptx" gives "pneumothorax" and "PTX" gives "PNEUMOTHORAX". A match inside a longer word is left alone. This is the only mode that can fire live while you speak.
  • Exactly as written — character for character, capitals included. Best for a boilerplate sentence you want removed verbatim.
  • Any sentence containing ALL of these words — matches a whole sentence however it is worded, so one rule covers a family of near-identical drafted statements instead of half a dozen entries.

The line under the list tells you what is actually running: how many rules act on drafted text, how many on your dictation, and how many of those fire live as you type. It turns amber when nothing is switched on.

Tips & gotchas

  • Order matters. Rules run top to bottom and each one sees the previous one's output. Use Move up and Move down to sort them.
  • Only whole-word rules can run live. The two sentence modes need a finished sentence, so they run at Process Report rather than while you are still speaking. The editor says so next to the choice.
  • Drafted rules need their switch. Rules aimed at the AI's draft do nothing until "Apply rules to drafted text" is ticked. Rules aimed at your own dictation are not gated by it.
  • A rule aimed nowhere cannot be saved — the editor will not let you leave both "applies to" boxes unticked, because such a rule would sit in the list looking live and never fire.
  • Your old lists are still written to the settings file, so rolling back to an older build finds your rules where that build looks for them.
