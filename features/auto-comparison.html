Automatic Comparison

Puts the COMPARISON line at the top of your transcript by itself as each study opens — from Clario's prior list, or from the study actually loaded in InteleViewer — and can fold a run of repeat films into one sentence.

What it does

As a study opens, MosaicTools writes a COMPARISON: line at the top of your transcript so you do not have to dictate one. Everything that can produce that line — this, Get Prior, and your own typing — folds into the same single line, newest study first. A report can never grow two comparison lines.

If a comparison line is already there when the study opens, it stands down. It will never duplicate a comparison macro of your own.

The two sources

Insert most recent prior comparison on open. Reads Clario's list of the patient's priors and picks the most recent relevant one. Clario knows which priors exist.

Read the comparison from the InteleViewer study list. Reads InteleViewer's study list off the screen, once per study, to find which prior is actually loaded — the one you are looking at. Clario cannot tell you that.

This second one is no longer developer-only as of 6.0.6, but it is still off unless you turn it on. It reads the screen once per study, never continuously, and if anything is unclear — the panel is not where it is expected, a date will not read, two rows disagree — it writes nothing. A wrong comparison is worse than no comparison.

Get Prior always wins: pulling the prior by hand replaces the automatic line with the fuller one.

Combine repeat studies into one statement

New in 6.0.6, a child of the InteleViewer setting above, and off by default.

A patient with a run of repeat films produces a list:

COMPARISON: 7/6/2026, 3/19/2026 chest radiograph 1 view, 6/13/2025 chest radiograph 1 view,
3/12/2025 chest radiograph 2 views, 3/9/2025 chest radiograph 2 views.

With this on, that becomes one sentence:

COMPARISON: chest radiographs from 7/6/2026, 3/19/2026, 6/13/2025, 3/12/2025, 3/9/2025.

What counts as the same study:

  • View counts do not split a group. A 1-view and a 2-view chest film are one study here.
  • Neither does contrast wording, or portable versus standard. When every prior words it the same way the wording is kept; when they differ it is dropped rather than one prior's wording being applied to the others.
  • Rib films group with chest films. A rib CT does not — the rule is for plain films.
  • A wider prior covers a narrower one. A chest/abdomen/pelvis CT angiogram and a chest CT angiogram are both at least chest CT angiograms, so they combine under the narrower name.
  • A prior with no description still counts. Its date joins the list.

When the priors are genuinely different studies it writes one statement per kind. When the shared name is only part of the truth — some angiograms, some plain — it says so: chest CT studies from ….

It also folds in a prior you pull with Get Prior part-way through the case, on the spot.

What counts as technique, not a study 6.0.7

A comparison line names WHICH priors you compared to. How the film was taken is not part of that, so these all drop out of a combined name:

  • view counts — "1 view", "2 views", "single view", "3 or more views"
  • PA and lateral, and other view pairs
  • portable, upright, erect, supine
  • adult, pediatric — and stat, routine
  • the reconstruction note ("+Recon", "and reconstructions")
  • contrast wording, when the priors disagree about it

⚠️ "limited" and "screening" are deliberately kept. Those change what the study IS — a limited ultrasound and a complete one are different examinations — and folding them together would claim a comparison you never made. A lone "lateral" is kept too: on its own it is a location, not a view.

Two more things that used to reach the report and no longer do: InteleViewer's timezone stamp ("00:41 EDT"), and a bracketed repeat of the body part ("chest radiographs (chest)").

The right study on a busy day 6.0.8

When InteleViewer showed a CT and a radiograph on the same date and only the CT was loaded, MosaicTools picked the CT correctly and then wrote it up as a radiograph.

It looks up proper wording in the worklist, and the worklist held only the radiograph for that day — it only carries what came through it, while InteleViewer shows the whole PACS history. With exactly one study on that date, MosaicTools took it without checking anything.

A lone same-day match now has to agree on modality before its wording is used. If it disagrees, MosaicTools falls back to the viewer's own text or a bare date — never another study's description.

When it leaves your line alone

Deliberately, and without saying anything:

  • text on the line that carries no date, because that is something you typed
  • a two-digit year, which cannot be reprinted without inventing a century
  • priors whose body parts merely overlap, rather than one containing the other
  • a different modality — a chest radiograph is never folded into a chest CT
  • nothing actually repeating, so there is nothing to gain

How to turn it on

Settings → Drafting → Prior comparison. Tick the source you want, then tick Combine repeat studies into one statement underneath the InteleViewer one if you want the shorter wording.
