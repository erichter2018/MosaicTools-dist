Unprocessed-Sign Warning

Beeps and shows a red warning box when you press Sign while dictation is still sitting unprocessed in the transcript box — press Sign again to sign anyway, or Process Report to capture it first.

What it does

The failure mode this prevents: you dictate a late addition, forget to re-run Process Report, and sign — the report goes out missing your last sentences. When you trigger Sign Report (Alt+F in Mosaic), MosaicTools first checks whether the transcript box looks unprocessed. If it does, it beeps and shows a red, movable warning box instead of signing.

It distinguishes two cases and words the box accordingly: dictation that was never processed ("may not be in the report"), and dictation that changed after you processed — a late edit or a streaming correction that arrived after the fact ("Press Process Report again to capture it").

How to turn it on / set it up

Settings → Behavior → Warn before signing (unprocessed transcript). Sign Report itself is mapped under Keys & Buttons.

Using it

The box never steals your keyboard — it's dismissed through your normal actions:

  • Press Sign again — signs anyway (you've decided the transcript remainder doesn't matter).
  • Press Process Report — processes the outstanding dictation first; then sign.
  • Start dictating again — the box closes and gets out of your way.

It's draggable and remembers its position between sessions.

Tips & gotchas

  • MosaicTools' own inserts into the transcript (a macro, a corrected clinical history) don't count as unprocessed dictation — only text you actually dictated or edited triggers it, which keeps false warnings rare.
  • Improved in 5.9.5. Two timing problems were making it warn on studies that had already been processed: on a study open, the record of what had been processed could be discarded a few seconds after it was made, and a macro landing while the report was still being processed was counted as new dictation. Both are fixed, so the warning now shows up when there really is something unprocessed.
  • The two-press pattern means a determined double-tap of Sign still signs quickly; the warning costs you one keypress exactly when it might save an addendum.
  • The alert tone was made deliberately audible (tuned in 5.1.x) — if you sign and hear a beep, look before pressing again.
